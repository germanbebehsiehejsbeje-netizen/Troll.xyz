Where on earth do you get the nerve
to act like a judge
passing judgment on others
while never accepting any questioning yourself?
You stand there
as if you hold the truth in your hands,
yet in reality you haven’t even sorted out the most basic logic.
The way you speak
makes it seem like the whole world owes you recognition,
but the problem is
you don’t even have the ability to earn that recognition.
All your confidence
is built upon a wall of fiction,
and on that wall
there is no foundation,
only the empty fantasies you’ve woven yourself.
Your so-called “rationality”
is nothing but finely packaged prejudice.
Your so-called “objectivity”
is merely the angle that benefits you.
The moment someone touches your blind spot,
you immediately start avoiding it,
changing the subject,
or even denying the other person’s character.
This is not debate.
This is guilt and insecurity.
You simply don’t dare to face the limits of your own vision,
so you always attack others
to cover up your own weaknesses.
Your heart is filled with anxiety,
and the toughness you display
is nothing but a disguise.
What you’re best at
is not analyzing problems,
but labeling others.
You replace complex thinking
with simple and crude judgments.
With a few words you think are sharp,
you cover up the poverty of your own understanding.
You refuse to admit that the world is more complicated than you imagine,
because that would mean you need to learn,
that you need to admit you’re not as clever as you think.
But you can’t do it.
You would rather maintain a false sense of superiority
than face your real shortcomings.
You would rather tear others down completely
than admit you’re on the same level as them.
You always stand after the fact, pointing fingers and commenting,
yet you’ve never taken any risks during the process.
You always give grand speeches after others make mistakes,
yet you’ve never stepped up to take responsibility at critical moments.
You say others aren’t working hard enough,
but you never consider the effort they put in behind the scenes.
You only see the surface of their failure,
never the hard work behind it.
You habitually criticize others,
but never reflect on yourself,
because you only want to see others make a fool of themselves,
never seriously examining what you yourself have done.
Watching safely from the sidelines
and then commenting from a high position —
this is the posture you’re most skilled at.
You’re afraid of facing your own ignorance.
You’re afraid of facing the flaws you don’t want to admit.
So you choose to criticize others
to cover up your own emptiness and unease.
You think you’re a “wise” bystander,
but in fact you’re just a self-important pretender.
People with real ability
never indulge in this kind of cheap sense of superiority.
They’re busy doing things,
busy solving problems,
busy making things better.
While you
are busy proving that others can’t do it.
You use the simplest and most primitive methods
to deny other people’s efforts,
yet you never take the initiative to change anything yourself.
You only use blame to maintain your meager sense of existence.
This mentality will sooner or later get you abandoned by the world.
You think pointing out problems makes you superior,
but pointing out problems is easy.
What’s hard is offering better solutions.
You never provide solutions
because you don’t have any.
You only have criticism,
but no construction.
You only have negation,
but no creation.
You always look for value in other people’s mistakes,
but never try to create value of your own.
You can’t understand
that real value lies in innovation,
not in consuming the wreckage left by others.
You’re used to packaging yourself as someone who is “awake,”
as if everyone else is drunk and only you are sober.
But the reality is
you’re just someone who refuses to grow.
Growth means correction,
means admitting mistakes,
means overthrowing your old self.
And you
are afraid of all that.
You’re always trapped in a cycle of self-limitation,
always thinking your “correctness” is enough to cover everything,
yet you never reflect:
when you firmly believe in your own “correctness,”
is it possible that you’re completely wrong?
You’re afraid of being surpassed,
afraid of being exposed,
afraid of having your real ability boundaries seen.
So you choose to preemptively deny others,
as if that could secure your own position.
But in fact,
what you truly expose
is your lack of confidence.
You need to constantly make yourself look strong
in order to cover up the fragility inside you.
But this method only treats the symptoms, not the root cause.
It makes you drift further and further away from true strength.
You always think others aren’t hardworking enough,
aren’t smart enough,
aren’t mature enough.
But have you ever seriously asked yourself:
Are you really better than them?
In what way are you better?
What gives you the right to be better?
What do you rely on to be better?
If the answer is just a few empty judgments,
then unfortunately,
that’s not strength —
that’s just bluffing.
Your so-called “excellence”
is nothing but a sandcastle built by stepping on others.
You have never truly asked yourself
whether you deserve to be looked up to,
whether you really have the ability to earn everyone’s respect.
The most exhausting thing about you
is not your criticism,
but the fact that you never take responsibility.
You don’t take consequences,
you don’t take responsibility,
you don’t take emotions,
yet you righteously demand that others bear the pressure you create.
You point your sharp edge at others,
but never allow others to point it at you.
You always demand that others follow the standards you set,
yet you never follow any rules yourself.
You’re not incapable of respect —
you’re unwilling to respect.
Because respect means equality,
and what you need is superiority.
Once you lose that superiority,
you no longer know how to define yourself.
You think others admire you,
but it’s only because you always show off and intimidate them.
Once the spotlight in the crowd disappears,
you will find that you actually have nothing.
So you desperately maintain that high posture,
even though it stands in mid-air with no foundation.
You would rather look strong
than actually be strong.
You are like a fragile glass ball
wrapped in a shiny golden surface.
But with even the slightest impact,
you would shatter into pieces.
You may still think you’ve won right now —
won because your voice is louder,
your attitude is tougher,
your words are harsher.
But real winning and losing
has never been about momentum —
it’s about time.
Time will filter out truly valuable ideas
and also filter out empty noise.
When the noise fades away,
when emotions leave the stage,
what remains
is only the content.
And at that time,
you will discover
that you have never truly built anything.
You have only opposed,
denied,
and dismantled,
but never created.
You want others to look up to you,
yet you have never reached a height worth looking up to.
You want others to be convinced,
yet you have never shown convincing ability.
You want others to shut up,
yet you have never used facts to make them silent.
True sharpness
is not about hurting people,
but about precision.
True strength
is not about suppression,
but about transcendence.
And you
are still stuck in the stage of using tone instead of substance.
Perhaps one day,
when you are willing to let go of that false sense of superiority,
truly start learning,
truly start taking responsibility,
and truly start facing your own shortcomings,
you will discover
that you don’t need to step on others
to stand steadily.
But until then,
all your high and mighty attitudes
only expose one fact —
You are far from being as remarkable as you imagine.
